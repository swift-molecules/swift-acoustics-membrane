# swift-acoustics-membrane

Use this package when a sound comes from a round stretched head, such as a kettledrum's: its modes, their frequencies under the air's load, their masses, and what they radiate. Start from `Acoustics.Membrane`.

## Laws

- A membrane's mode has `diameters` and `circles`, the lines that stay still across it and around it (the rim counts as a circle), and `zero`, the zero of the Bessel function that sets it. `modes(below:in:)` gives every mode sounding under a frequency, by diameters and then circles. Each mode with diameters stands for two, a quarter of a turn apart around the head.
- A mode's `frequency(of:in:)` is its wavenumber, zero / radius, times √(tension / moved mass) over 2π. The moved mass per area is the head's `density` plus `load` × the air's density / wavenumber: the air that moves with the head, as a first approximation in one fitted number. With no load it is the head in a vacuum. With a load of 3.95 the head measured by Christian and others in 1984 (0.32 m, 5360 N/m, 0.262 kg/m²; as given by Packer, Durham thesis 1993, tables 7.1 and 7.2) has its (1,1) mode at 172 Hz and the three above it within 3.5% of the measured 1.50, 1.97 and 2.44 times that.
- `mass(of:in:)` is the moved mass per area times the integral of the squared shape over the head; `shape(of:at:)` is the shape along a radius where it is largest around the head, and zero outside the rim; `patch(of:across:)` is how much of the mode a round patch of that radius feels against a point, 2 J₁(wavenumber × radius) over wavenumber × radius; `stretch(of:)` is the integral of the squared slope of the shape.
- `beam(of:at:tilted:in:)` is the far sound pressure times distance for a unit acceleration of the mode, at an angle from straight above the head, for a head set in a wall (Rayleigh's integral), in the direction around the head where the mode is loudest. A listener at another angle around hears it times the cosine of diameters × that angle, and a quarter of a cycle later for each diameter. Straight above, only modes without diameters are heard.
- `radiated(by:at:in:)` is the decay a mode has from the sound it sends out alone, its power over the half space above over twice its energy, by 200 slices of the angle. For the head above it gives ring times within a factor of two of the ones Christian and others calculated for the first two modes with one circle and the first two with two (Packer's table 6.1).
- Every value is a quantity of its kind (radius a length, density a surface density, frequencies, wavenumbers, masses and decays), except three no atom holds yet: `tension` in N/m, `beam` in Pa·m per m/s², and the pure numbers `load`, `shape`, `patch` and `stretch`.
